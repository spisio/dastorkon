No dastorkon is laid without boorsok. My grandmother said the smell of the frying fat at a memorial reaches the ones we have lost, and she always fried a batch on the seventh day.

Kyrgyz and Kazakh families make them the same way. Some cut diamonds, some squares; our village cut squares.

<!--method-->

## Method

1. Stir the yeast and sugar into the warm milk and leave 10 minutes until foamy.
2. Add the egg, melted butter and salt, then the flour. Knead 8–10 minutes into a soft, smooth dough.
3. Cover and leave to rise in a warm place for about 1 hour, until doubled.
4. Roll out on a floured board to just under 1 cm thick. Cut into 3–4 cm squares. Cut a small slit in the centre of a few so they puff evenly.
5. Heat the oil to 175 °C. Fry a handful at a time, turning once, for 1–2 minutes until puffed and golden.
6. Drain on paper. Serve warm or cold, piled high, with tea, honey and kaymak.
