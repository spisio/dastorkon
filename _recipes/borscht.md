In Nurzhan every Russian-speaking grandmother has her own borscht, and my friend Olga's mother used to ladle it out for half the building on cold October evenings. I learned it in her kitchen on holidays from university in Talas, and I still make it the way she did.

Borscht came to us with the Soviet years and stayed. In the cities it is now an ordinary weeknight soup, and in Nurzhan it is a common name-day dish. We make it with beef, never anything else, and I put a dollop of qatiq on top when I have no sour cream.

## Method

1. Put the beef, water, whole onion, bay leaves and 1.5 tsp salt in a large pot. Bring to a boil, skim off the foam, then simmer gently, partly covered, for 1 h 30 min until the meat is tender.
2. Lift out the meat and the onion. Discard the onion. Cut the meat off the bones into bite-size pieces and return it to the broth.
3. Heat the oil in a wide pan over medium heat. Fry the chopped onion and carrot for 5 min until soft.
4. Add the beetroot and the vinegar and fry for 5 min, stirring. Stir in the tomato paste and fry 2 min more, then add a ladle of hot broth and simmer 5 min.
5. Add the potatoes to the broth and cook for 10 min.
6. Add the cabbage and the beetroot mixture. Simmer for 15 min until the potatoes and cabbage are soft.
7. Season with salt and pepper, stir in the garlic, turn off the heat and let the pot stand covered for 15 min.
8. Serve hot with sour cream, dill and bread.
